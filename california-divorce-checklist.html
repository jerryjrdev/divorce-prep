California Divorce Checklist 2026

Everything you need to file, disclose, and finalize a divorce in California — from the FL-100 petition to the final judgment.

How divorce works in California

California is a no-fault state: you file for "irreconcilable differences" and the court does not assign blame. California is also a community property state — assets and debts acquired during the marriage are generally split 50/50.

There is a mandatory 6-month waiting period from the date your spouse is served (or files a response) before the divorce can be finalized, even if you both agree on everything.

The California divorce checklist

  1. Gather personal identification documents — IDs, marriage certificate, Social Security numbers for both spouses.
  2. Complete the petition (FL-100) and summons (FL-110). File them at your county superior court and pay the filing fee (or request a fee waiver, FW-001).
  3. Serve your spouse — someone over 18 who is not you must deliver the papers; then file the proof of service (FL-115). Your spouse has 30 days to respond (FL-120).
  4. File Preliminary Declarations of Disclosure — each spouse must exchange an FL-140, income and expense declaration (FL-150), and a schedule of assets and debts (FL-142) within 60 days of filing.
  5. Inventory community and separate property — everything acquired during marriage vs. before marriage, by gift, or by inheritance.
  6. Track expenses while the case is open, marking marital vs. separate spending.
  7. Draft your settlement — asset split, spousal support, and any parenting plan. Use a marital settlement agreement (or form FL-180 paperwork for default).
  8. Dividing retirement accounts usually requires a QDRO filed with the court.
  9. Wait out the 6-month clock from service.
  10. File the judgment (FL-180) and receive the stamped final judgment.
