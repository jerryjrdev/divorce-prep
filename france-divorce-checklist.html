Divorce in France: The Complete Checklist

Most French divorces are by mutual consent — no judge, just lawyers and a notaire. Here is the full process and the deadlines.

How divorce works in France

French law recognizes several divorce types: divorce par consentement mutuel (mutual consent — no court hearing), divorce by accepted breakdown of the marriage, divorce for definitive alteration of the marital bond (after one year of separation), and fault-based divorce.

Since 2017, a mutual-consent divorce is handled by the spouses' lawyers and registered with a notaire — no judge is involved.

The French divorce checklist

  1. Choose your divorce type — mutual consent is fastest when you agree on everything.
  2. Each spouse engages an avocat — separate lawyers are mandatory, even in mutual consent.
  3. Negotiate the convention de divorce — asset division, the prestation compensatoire (compensatory payment), child residence, and support.
  4. Respect the 15-day reflection period — after receiving the draft convention, both spouses must wait 15 days before signing.
  5. Sign the convention — together with both lawyers.
  6. File with the notaire — the notaire registers the convention within 15 days of signature. The divorce takes effect on registration.
  7. For contested divorces — the lawyer files the assignation with the family judge (JAF); expect a longer, court-led process.
  8. Update your records — livret de famille, banks, tax status, pensions, and inheritance documents.

How long does it take?

A mutual-consent divorce typically completes in 1 to 3 months after the convention is ready. Contested divorces take a year or more.
