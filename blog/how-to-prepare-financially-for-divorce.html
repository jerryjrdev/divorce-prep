How to Prepare Financially for Divorce: 12 Steps

The spouse with the organized financial picture negotiates from strength. Here is your 12-step plan.

Why financial preparation matters

Money questions dominate divorce: who keeps the house, how retirement accounts split, what support looks like. The spouse who shows up with a complete, organized financial picture saves billable hours, reduces anxiety, and negotiates from strength.

The 12 steps

  1. Collect statements — bank, investment, retirement, mortgage, and credit card statements for the last 12 months.
  2. Gather key documents — tax returns (2–3 years), pay stubs, deeds, car titles, and the marriage certificate.
  3. List every asset — home, vehicles, accounts, retirement plans, businesses, valuables. Note an approximate value and whether each is marital or separate.
  4. List every debt — mortgages, loans, credit cards. Note whose name is on each.
  5. Compute your net worth — assets minus debts. This single number frames every negotiation.
  6. Track your monthly spending — separate "marital" spending from "separate" spending once you know your jurisdiction's rules.
  7. Open your own accounts — a personal checking account and a credit card in your name, before joint accounts close.
  8. Check your credit — pull your report and watch for changes.
  9. Understand retirement division — in many jurisdictions a court order (like a QDRO in the US) is needed to split retirement accounts.
  10. Estimate support — most jurisdictions use income-based formulas; test scenarios with a calculator.
  11. Protect documents — store everything on your own device, backed up, and locked.
  12. Prepare questions for your lawyer — write them down before the consultation; billable time is precious.
