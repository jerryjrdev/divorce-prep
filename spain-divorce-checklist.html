Divorce in Spain: The Complete Checklist

Spanish divorce needs no cause — but there is a 3-month minimum. Here is the full process, from the convenio regulador to the sentencia.

How divorce works in Spain

Spain is a no-fault system: the only requirement is that three months have passed since the wedding (waived in cases of risk or violence). Divorce is by mutuo acuerdo (mutual consent, with a convenio regulador agreement) or contencioso (contested, decided by a judge).

In most regions the default marital regime is sociedad de gananciales — community property of the marriage.

The Spanish divorce checklist

  1. Confirm the 3-month rule — the marriage must be at least three months old.
  2. Gather documents — marriage certificate (certificado de matrimonio), birth certificates of children, padrón registration, and financial records.
  3. Mutual consent: draft the convenio regulador — asset division, pensión compensatoria (compensatory pension), child custody, and use of the family home.
  4. File jointly with your abogado and procurador — both spouses sign the demanda de divorcio together.
  5. Contested route — one spouse files the demanda; the judge decides assets, custody, and support.
  6. Attend the hearing if required.
  7. Receive the sentencia de divorcio — the divorce decree.
  8. Register the divorce — it is recorded in the civil registry, and the marriage certificate is annotated.
  9. Update your records — tax status, banks, wills, and pensions.

How long does it take?

A mutual-consent divorce can complete in a few months depending on the court. Contested divorces take longer.
